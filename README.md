# space-sim

Top-down 2D space trading sim with thrust-only ships, Keplerian orbits, a living
economy and sub-light cryo travel between stars. Written in Odin with raylib;
art is fastart (`.fart`) vector documents.

Design: [docs/DESIGN.md](docs/DESIGN.md).

## Build

```
make run        # debug build and run
make release    # optimised binary in bin/
make test       # odin test over all packages
```

Requires Odin (`brew install odin`) and make. Run from the repo root so
`assets/` resolves.

The game opens on the start menu, over a sky that is somewhere rather than
nothing: a nebula field baked from thousands of puffs, filaments and dust
lanes, a distant sun, a ringed gas giant with a moon and a habitat wheel, and
traffic crossing on its own errands with the odd comet behind it
(`src/render/menu_sky.odin`). **New game** takes a galaxy seed (or
leaves it to chance), a galaxy shape and size, and a starting hull; the ship
is free and you begin with 5,000 credits. **Load game** and **Save game** use
five named slots plus a quick slot (F5/F9). **Settings** has Graphics (star
glow, background stars, planet shading, effects, vsync), Display (windowed,
borderless or fullscreen and the window size), Sound (master, music and
interface volumes) and Controls, where every key can be rebound by clicking
it and pressing a new one; conflicts show in red. Settings persist in
`settings.json`. In a game, System > Main menu returns to the menu with the
game paused underneath.

**People.** Every trader has a pilot and every station a vendor: a seeded
name, a personality (gruff, cheerful, nervous, formal or sly) and a face.
Hover a trader and Plot course to rendezvous with it; within docking range,
Ask to dock. Pilots can refuse for the day depending on their temper. Docked,
you ride their orbit while they wait, and Talk opens a conversation: small
talk, rumours (real prices and shortages), their cargo, and trading. Pilots
sell their cargo a little under what it fetches at their destination and buy
the same commodity a little over what they paid, and you may ask once for a
better price. The market window shows the station's vendor with a greeting
and a Talk button. Lines come from `assets/dialog/lines.json`, written by
hand in `tools/dialog_lines.py` (about 270 lines with slots for names, systems,
commodities and prices).

Ship hulls are generated by `tools/gen_ships.py`: brutalist slabs and
stacked modules in monochrome greys (`tools/preview_ships.py` renders a
sheet of them without the game).

Stations come from `tools/gen_stations.py` in the same language, one document
per kind so a yard reads as a yard: a berthing drum for the hub, an open
gantry for the shipyard, tankage and a flare for the refinery, racked
containers for the depot and a spun ring for the habitat. Every document fits
a fixed envelope (`core.STATION_DOC_HALF`) so one scale sizes all five.
`tools/preview_stations.py` sheets them beside a courier and a freighter at
the true world-space ratio.

Hulls are animated by fastart clips, not sprite sheets. Under thrust the
exhaust burns blue-white and flickers, the plume growing with the throttle
and hanging off the engine bell, which gimbals a few degrees into a turn.
Turning fires attitude jets: the bow jet on the side *away* from the turn
and the stern jet opposite it, the couple that swings the nose. The turn is
read from the heading itself, so a hand turn (arrow keys), a prograde hold
and the autopilot all fire them, and traders get them too.

NPC faces are fastart too: `tools/gen_avatars.py` writes one document per
feature (heads, hair with back and front layers, eyes, brows, noses, mouths,
facial hair, collars, extras) into `assets/avatars`, and the game layers and
recolours them per person from a seed (`--avatars` shows a sheet of faces).

Music and interface sounds are synthesised by `tools/gen_audio.py` into
`assets/audio` (regenerate them with `python3 tools/gen_audio.py`).

Once in a game the view follows the ship in its orbit; `H` frames the whole system.

## Controls

The toolbar runs across the top: **Orders** (go to the hovered target,
choose a route, fly to a point, orbit here at an altitude, dock and undock,
automatic time and docking), **View** (follow, frame, draw toggles, galaxy map), **Trade**
(market, known routes, shipyard, your contracts), **System** (save/load,
regenerate, seeds, debug panel) and **Manual** (throttle, holds, turning and
maneuver nodes for anyone who wants the stick), with a Contacts toggle, the time step, pause and the clock on the
right. Time runs as a step of game time per real second: 1 s (real time),
1 min, 10 min, 30 min, 1 hour, 1 day, 1 month or 1 year. `-` and `+` (or `,`
and `.`) move through the steps, and clicking the step opens the full list.
Burns hold the step down to a few seconds per second while the engine is lit. A minimap of the system sits top right with a **Refocus ship** button.

The flight instruments sit across the bottom centre and are read as gauges
rather than as a paragraph: throttle, propellant and dv as vertical bars with
their numbers boxed underneath; a scope in the middle drawing the conic you
are actually on around the body you are bound to, with periapsis and apoapsis
marked, the sphere of influence when it fits, and your ship as a chevron
pointing along its velocity; periapsis, apoapsis, eccentricity (as a bar from
circle to escape) and period beside it; and altitude and speed as tapes with
Pe and Ap marked on one and circular and escape speed on the other, so every
number has a reference sitting next to it. The header says what the ship is
doing and to what, and lights the lamps: hold, autoburn, an armed node, a
damaged hull and whatever hazard you are sitting in - which also pulses the
frame red. Live prompts (docking range, the autopilot, the next event) stack
just above the panel, and the instruments stand down while the market or the
shipyard is open over them.

The ship's log fills the bottom left: warnings, arrivals, docks, burns and
failures with the time each happened at, and the orbit written down whenever
the ship settles into a new one - after a burn, crossing into another sphere
of influence, or arriving in a system. It keeps the last two hundred lines and
the wheel scrolls it back.

Ships face the way they fly: prograde while coasting, along the burn while
the autopilot fires. Turning by hand (Left/Right) keeps your heading until
you press a hold or Release hold, or the next autoburn takes the stick.

The camera follows your ship. Drag or use WASD to look elsewhere, click the
minimap to jump there, and Refocus (or `F`) to return. `Q` and `E` rotate the
view, `Home` resets it, and `V` (View > Lock view to ship heading) keeps the
ship pointing up as it turns; the minimap box shows the rotated view. Execute
burn moved to `B`. Nothing needs selecting:
hover any planet, moon, station or ship and a popover shows a picture, a
summary and the actions that apply, such as Plot course, Dock, Trade, Shipyard
and Look at. Actions that need proximity say so until you are close enough.

**Time runs itself.** While the autopilot flies, the clock speeds up to the
next burn or event on its own, paced by the leg: each coast takes at least a
dozen real seconds so you watch the ship move, never faster than the ceiling
shown on the bar, and it drops back for burns. The bar's **auto** button turns that off; **> burn** and **> arrival**
jump straight to the next burn or the arrival. Things worth a decision stop
the clock with a card: arriving, entering a star's heat line or a pulsar
wind, and a passing trader hailing you (answer for a chat over the radio).

**Jobs.** Every station and colony posts its own jobs, picked up on the spot:
hover it and choose Jobs, press Jobs on its market window, or ask the vendor
"Any work going?". Trade > Your contracts lists what you hold. Jobs are
deliveries whose goods
are handed to you on acceptance, procurement for goods the destination is
short of, and passengers. Each pays on delivery and has a deadline on the
game clock, so the choice between a cheap route and a fast one matters. You
can hold three at once; arriving at the destination with the goods aboard
completes the job automatically.

Trade windows always open; when no station is in range they say so. Planets
with a **colony** trade too, but nothing docks: park in a low orbit and the
market queues your orders on a shuttle that ferries ten units at a time down
and back, a few hours per round trip. You can watch it fly, and it pauses if
you leave orbit. Trading
works while docked or within docking range. Movement is by order: hover a planet, station or ship and press **Go** (it
takes the balanced route and flies), **Go and dock** or **Rendezvous**, or
**Choose route...** to see the alternatives. **Orbit here at...** raises or
lowers your orbit around the body you are circling, in two burns plus a
trim. Ships dock by themselves when they arrive in range at matched speed
(Orders > Dock automatically). For the last few units, Shift + arrows give
tiny RCS nudges. You can also grab the Pe or Ap marker on your orbit and
drag it: the burn at the opposite apsis is set up and armed when you let
go. Maneuver nodes with their sliders remain under Manual. Ships have a hull (shown in the readout). Three things wear it down so far:
sitting inside a star's heat line (a faint red ring around every star), a
pulsar's wind (the blue haze its beams sweep), and the dust of a nebula.
Cross a star's surface, or run
the hull to zero, and the ship is destroyed. Star systems come in seven kinds,
from brown dwarfs to red supergiants, neutron stars and pulsars; the galaxy map
names the kind and warns about the dangerous ones.

**Nebulae.** About one system in six holds a cloud of gas, and which kind it
is follows from the star it surrounds: a supernova remnant around a pulsar or
a neutron star, a planetary nebula shed by a white dwarf, an emission nebula
lit by hot blue stars, a blue reflection veil of dust, or a star nursery still
condensing. Remnants and planetary nebulae are hollow shells with the star in
the middle; the rest are clouds off to one side of the system. Some are large
enough to be a destination in their own right: the galaxy map draws those as
coloured gas rather than a star, and they have no planets and no stations —
just the cloud and the survey ships working it.

Fly into one and **Orders > Skim the nebula** puts the scoop out. Every pass
takes a couple of game hours and strains out what the gas is made of —
hydrogen and ices from a nursery, oxygen from an emission nebula, silicate
grit from a reflection nebula, heavy metals from a supernova shell — and tops
the propellant tank from the raw hydrogen, which is the only way to refuel
with no station in reach. The panel shows the pass timer, how thick the gas is
where you are sitting, and what has come aboard; drift out of the cloud and
the timer holds until you come back. Dust scours the hull the whole time,
faster in a remnant, so the wait costs something. Survey ships are built for
it and yours is not.

Interstellar travel starts on the galaxy map (**View > Galaxy
map**): pick a linked system and press its Jump button. Still deep in the
system, the button flies you out past the star's grip first and a prompt asks
you to engage cryo once you are clear. Keyboard shortcuts are shown beside menu items; the continuous
controls are keyboard only: hold `←`/`→` to turn and `↑`/`↓` for throttle;
wheel zooms, right-drag or WASD pans; drag a node along its path to retime it.

Fonts: the game bundles any `.ttf`/`.otf` dropped into `assets/fonts/`, else
uses a system font (Verdana/Arial on macOS, DejaVu on Linux).

Edit any file under `assets/` while the game runs; it hot-reloads. Planet
documents are generated by `python3 tools/gen_planets.py` (several variants per
kind, recoloured per planet in game); lighting, limb darkening and atmosphere
rims are drawn by the renderer on top. `View > Trade routes` draws the current
route table between stations; a legend of line kinds sits at the top.

`./bin/space-sim --seed 7` picks a system. For smoke tests,
`--screenshot out.png [--frames N] [--panel] [--overview] [--burn] [--flyby] [--node T,PRO,RAD] [--execute] [--dest NAME --objective fuel|time|balanced|simplest --autowarp --warpcap W --until-done] [--menu N] [--system N] [--cryo N|near] [--map N|near [--jump]] [--stars] [--mapzoom Z] [--play] [--angle DEG] [--headlock] [--avatars] [--talk vendor|pilot|trade] [--colony] [--jobs] [--orbit ALT [--orbit-go]] [--title home|new|load|save|settings|controls|sound] [--nebulae] [--skim] [--doom --until-dead] [--warp I] [--trace] [--focus NAME] [--zoom Z] [--time T] [--turn ±1]`
renders and exits.
